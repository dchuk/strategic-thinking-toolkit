# Run Mutation Game

**Purpose:** Facilitate a full 60-minute Mutation Game session to generate a wide range of strategic options for a business object. Use this command whenever you need to produce many innovation candidates quickly by systematically varying the components of a descriptive sentence — the third source of strategic ideas alongside copying competitors and asking customers.

**When to use:**
- You need to generate strategic options for a business object (process, product, industry, initiative, company) and are early in the Up phase
- The team has exhausted copying competitors and observing customers as ideation sources
- You need divergent thinking before narrowing down to a shortlist
- A Pyramid Principle or Happy Line session has surfaced a gap but has not yet produced concrete options to fill it

---

## Steps

### 0. Identify the business object (2 mins)

Ask the user: *What is the business object you want to mutate?* (e.g., a customer onboarding process, a product line, a distribution channel, a service model). Confirm the scope before proceeding.

### 1. Craft a Description (5 mins)

1. Write a short descriptive sentence for the business object **as it stands today**.
2. Break it into **five to eight meaningful components** — one component per Post-it in a column. Avoid bridging words ('and', 'the'); every word in the grid must carry distinct meaning. Multi-word components are allowed when necessary (e.g., 'for a short ride').
3. Produce **two formulations**: one demand-side (starting from the customer perspective) and one supply-side (starting from the provider/expert perspective). Per the demand-side-preferred principle, the demand-side version usually expands thinking more — select it as the primary sentence, but keep the supply-side version for a second pass.
4. Review: if two components could be merged without losing meaning, merge them to keep the sentence compact.

Output: a single column of five to eight component Post-its representing the chosen sentence.

### 2. Generate Alternative Components (15 mins)

For each component in the description column, generate **two to four alternative variants** and place them in additional columns to the right. Rules:
- **Rule: alternatives-per-component** — two to four variants per row.
- **Rule: rectangular-grid** — aim for a full rectangle (five to eight rows × two to four columns) with as few blank spaces as possible.
- Silly or extreme variants are explicitly welcome at this stage — per the silly-ideas-are-fine principle, non-judgement preserves creative options that might trigger valuable mutations.
- Start large to stimulate creativity; you can prune impractical variants after the exploration phase (large-grid-then-shrink principle).

Output: a completed mutation grid displayed as a markdown table with one row per component and one column per variant (column 1 = original).

### 3. Pick Mutations and Generate Ideas (30 mins)

A **mutation** is formed by selecting one cell per row. Label each mutation with its **Mutation Unique Number**: a string of column-position digits, one digit per row (e.g., '112' means row 1 → col 1, row 2 → col 1, row 3 → col 2).

Exploration sequence (per start-with-few-variants rule):
1. **Warm-up (5 mins):** Vary only one or two components from the original (mostly column 1 selections). Record any ideas triggered.
2. **Loosen inhibitions (5 mins):** Pick combinations from the right side of the grid — many components replaced simultaneously. Record ideas; do not evaluate yet.
3. **Main sweep (20 mins):** Return to near-original combinations and systematically vary one or two components at a time. Target **~50 mutations at 10–20 seconds each**. Record all ideas, including multiple ideas per mutation. If a mutation yields nothing, move on immediately.

For each idea recorded, note: the idea text and the Mutation Unique Number that triggered it.

Output: a numbered list of raw ideas, each tagged with its Mutation Unique Number.

### 4. Package Your Best Ideas (10 mins)

1. Review the raw idea list. Select the most promising candidates.
2. Summarise each best idea in **two to four words**.
3. Attach the **Mutation Unique Number** of the triggering mutation to each packaged idea.
4. Present the packaged ideas as a clean shortlist.

Output: a shortlist table with columns: `#` | `Idea (2–4 words)` | `Mutation Number` | `One-sentence description`.

### 5. Optional — Second Pass with Supply-Side Sentence

If the first pass used the demand-side formulation, offer to repeat Steps 1–4 using the supply-side sentence. This can surface a complementary set of mutations.

---

## Verify

A successful Mutation Game session produces all of the following:

- [ ] A descriptive sentence of **five to eight components** (no bridging words) for the target business object.
- [ ] A **mutation grid** in rectangular form (5–8 rows × 2–4 columns, minimal blank cells).
- [ ] Evidence that **~50 mutations** were explored (the raw idea list should span many Mutation Unique Numbers).
- [ ] A **packaged shortlist** of best ideas, each summarised in two to four words and tagged with a Mutation Unique Number.
- [ ] At least one demand-side formulation was attempted.

If any checkpoint is missing, identify which step was incomplete and re-run from that step.

---

## Watch out for

- **Data-holic anti-pattern:** Do not pause to research or validate ideas during Steps 2–3. The Mutation Game is a divergent thinking technique — evaluation comes after, not during.
- **HiPPO Curse:** Ensure all participants generate variants and ideas freely. Dominant voices should not prune the grid during Step 2; silly ideas are fine.
- **Skipping the demand-side formulation:** Always attempt a demand-side sentence (Tip 3 / demand-side-preferred principle). Supply-side-only grids narrow the mutation space.
- **Grid too small:** A grid with fewer than five components or only one variant per component reduces the mutation count dramatically. Enforce the rectangular-grid rule before Step 3.
- **Stalling on a single mutation:** If a combination yields no idea within 20 seconds, move on immediately (more-mutations-more-survival principle — breadth matters more than depth per mutation).

## Notes

- The Mutation Game is an **Up phase** tool, sitting alongside the Pyramid Principle and Happy Line. Use it when you need to generate options, not evaluate them. See the phase-tool mapping in [../skills/strategic-thinking-toolkit/SKILL.md](../skills/strategic-thinking-toolkit/SKILL.md).
- For the step that selects *which* ideas to take forward into evaluation, use the Payoff Profiles Matrix (a Down phase tool). See [../skills/strategic-thinking-toolkit/references/tools/payoff-profiles.md](../skills/strategic-thinking-toolkit/references/tools/payoff-profiles.md).
