# Build Memorable Metrics

## Purpose
Construct a focused, behaviour-driving scorecard of no more than 12 Memorable Metrics — three per Balanced Scorecard perspective — for a new venture, initiative, or strategic review. Use this command whenever you need to define or reset KPIs so they are few, simple, and memorable enough to drive aligned behaviour.

## When to use
- Launching a new venture, initiative, or project and defining KPIs from scratch
- Resetting an existing dashboard that has grown too large or opaque
- All indicators on a current dashboard are bright green (Green-Dashboard Warning — see Rule below)
- Entering the Push phase of the Rollercoaster and needing Simple Numbers to anchor the story

## Steps

1. **Capture context.** Ask the user for the name of the venture or initiative, its desired future state, and whether any existing metrics or dashboards are in play. If a file or document exists, Read it now.

2. **Brainstorm candidates across all four perspectives.** Generate a long list (aim for 20–40) of possible metrics organised under the four Balanced Scorecard perspectives:
   - **Financial** — lagging indicators of past performance (revenue, margin, cost, cash)
   - **Customer** — leading indicators of future financial performance (satisfaction, retention, NPS, acquisition)
   - **Business Process** — operational measures that drive customer and financial outcomes (cycle time, quality, throughput)
   - **Learning & Growth** — capability and culture measures (skills, employee engagement, innovation rate)

   Present the full candidate list to the user, grouped by perspective, before proceeding.

3. **Clear the dashboard.** Announce that you are starting the bouncer method with zero metrics on the scorecard.

4. **Apply the bouncer method — one metric at a time.** For each round, ask: *"If you could only have one more metric on the scorecard, which would it be?"* Add the chosen metric and record its perspective. Repeat until the user either stops voluntarily or the scorecard reaches **12 metrics**. Enforce the cap — do not add a thirteenth metric.

5. **Check perspective balance.** Review the final 12 against the four perspectives. Flag any perspective with zero metrics and prompt the user to consider whether it is genuinely irrelevant or simply overlooked. Aim for approximately three metrics per perspective.

6. **Check for metric blend.** Verify the scorecard contains metrics across all three temporal horizons:
   - **Present management** — metrics that track current operational performance
   - **Better future outcomes** — metrics that signal progress toward the desired future state
   - **Aspirational / long-term behaviour change** — at least one metric that stretches behaviour over time

   If any horizon is missing, flag it and suggest candidate metrics from the brainstorm list.

7. **Design the glide path.** For each metric on the scorecard, ask: *"When this metric is consistently hitting target and becomes easy to meet, what metric would replace it?"* Document the planned successor metric. This pre-specifies how the scorecard evolves without requiring a full reset later.

8. **Assign ownership and measurement method.** For each of the 12 metrics, record:
   - Owner (person or function responsible)
   - Data source and update frequency
   - Target or direction of improvement

9. **Apply the Green-Dashboard Warning check.** Review the final scorecard and ask: *"If all 12 of these metrics were bright green simultaneously, would that genuinely mean the business is performing well — or are we missing something important?"* If the answer suggests blind spots, return to the candidate list and replace the weakest metrics.

10. **Present the completed scorecard.** Output a clean table with columns: Perspective | Metric | Owner | Data Source | Frequency | Glide-Path Successor.

## Verify
The command has succeeded when:
- The scorecard contains **no more than 12 metrics**, distributed across all four Balanced Scorecard perspectives
- At least one metric represents each temporal horizon (present, near-future, aspirational)
- Every metric has an owner, a data source, and a glide-path successor defined
- The Green-Dashboard Warning check has been applied and either passed or resolved
- The user confirms the set feels *few, simple, and memorable* enough to share without a slide deck

## Watch out for
- **Data-holic anti-pattern** — the urge to keep adding metrics "just in case". The bouncer method is the corrective: enforce strict priority order and the 12-metric cap.
- **Green-Dashboard Warning** — if all indicators are bright green, you are probably not measuring the right things. Revise rather than celebrate.
- **Rear-view mirror trap** — a scorecard dominated by Financial (lagging) metrics with no Customer or Learning & Growth (leading) measures will miss early warning signals of decline.
- **Missing glide path** — scorecards without planned successors become stale; the Netflix illustration (postal accuracy → server downtime → content inventory) shows how the critical metric changes as the business evolves.
