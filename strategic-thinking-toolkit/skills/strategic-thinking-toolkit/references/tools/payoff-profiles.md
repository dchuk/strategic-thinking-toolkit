# Payoff Profiles Matrix

## Problem Framing

You have a list of ten, twenty, maybe thirty strategic initiatives. Some are bold industry-shaping moonshots. Others are incremental table-stakes improvements. Senior leadership is spending the same meeting time debating a low-stakes hygiene project as a bet-the-company pivot — and no one has a principled way to decide which deserve investment, which should be killed, and which just need to be re-engineered.

The concrete pain: **20–30% of any company's initiatives typically land in the worst possible risk–reward combination** — high risk for minimal differentiated reward — and no one notices because the portfolio has never been mapped in one place. _source: ch12 §Destroy_

The Payoff Profiles Matrix is a **Down-phase tool** that solves this by putting every initiative on a single 3×3 grid, making imbalances visible, and prescribing four concrete decisions to rebalance the portfolio.

---

## The 3×3 Grid

The matrix has two axes:

- **Horizontal axis — Postures (Reward):** How much differentiated reward does this initiative offer?
  - **Shape** (left): seeks industry leadership, sets standards, creates demand
  - **Adapt** (centre): fast second-mover, captures existing opportunities through speed
  - **Reserve** (right): minimal investment to stay in the game without premature commitment

- **Vertical axis — Bets (Risk):** How much risk does this initiative carry?
  - **Big Bets** (top): potentially large positive or large negative payoff depending on context
  - **Options** (middle): small upfront cost, rare large payoff, frequent small/zero returns
  - **No Regrets** (bottom): positive outcome in any scenario

The nine cells are defined by combining one Posture with one Bet type. The **target cell** is **Shape Options** (top-left, moving one row down from Big Bets) — highest reward, manageable risk. The **danger cell** is **Reserve Big Bets** (top-right corner) — maximum risk for minimum reward.

---

## The Four Decisions

After every initiative is plotted, apply these four decisions in order:

### 1. Delegate (No Regret moves)
All initiatives in the **No Regrets** row must be delegated immediately to junior team members to execute and maximise their payoff. They should not occupy senior management attention. _source: ch12 §Delegate_

### 2. Destroy (Reserve Big Bets)
The **top-right corner must always be empty.** Any initiative there must be transformed:
- Move it **down** to Options by splitting it into successive stages invested in only after earlier stages succeed
- Move it **left** to Adapt by redesigning it to deliver greater, more differentiated benefit
- Move it **diagonally** (down and left simultaneously) by doing both

> Rule 1: The 'reserve big bets' cell must always be empty after a session closes. _source: ch12 §Destroy_

### 3. Discuss (Shape Big Bets)
Initiatives in the **Shape Big Bets** cell (top-left) warrant substantive senior management debate. Explore whether each can be re-engineered into an options-based approach before committing resources.

### 4. Drag (All Remaining Initiatives)
Every other initiative should be dragged as close as possible to the **Shape Options** box — reducing risk, increasing reward, or both. _source: ch12 §Drag_

## Glide Paths

A **Glide Path** is a multi-step trajectory that progressively re-engineers an initiative from a suboptimal position to a preferred one. Rather than expecting a single leap from Reserve Big Bets to Shape Options, you engineer a sequence of improved versions (V1 → V2 → V3), each plotted with a different Post-it colour, showing how risk decreases and reward increases over successive stages.

This embodies the principle: **move smart and think first.** Jumping ahead with the first version of an idea is "nearly always a guaranteed recipe for a sub-optimal outcome." _source: ch12 §Payoff Profiles exercise_

Glide paths are especially powerful for explaining portfolio evolution to stakeholders — each colour tells the story of improvement.

## Russian Dolls Quality

The Payoff Profiles Matrix has **Russian Dolls Quality**: it can be applied recursively at multiple organisational levels simultaneously.

- **Company level:** the full corporate initiative portfolio
- **Business-unit / departmental level:** one division's programme of work
- **Functional / team level:** a single team's project list

Each level produces its own 3×3 grid. Insights from the team level can surface upward; constraints from the company level can cascade downward. _source: ch12 §Payoff Profiles example_

## Facilitation Rules

**Rule 1: Plot quickly first.** Spend no more than 30 seconds on each initiative during the first pass. Use a rock-paper-scissors-style simultaneous vote on both axes. Plot initiatives with full consensus immediately; handle contested ones in a slower second round, comparing with already-plotted neighbours. _source: ch12 §The Payoff Profiles Matrix in practice, Tip 1_

**Rule 2: No pre-spoiling the Reserve Big Bets corner.** Do not explain the negative implications of the top-right corner before participants plot. Let them discover the problem organically — the optimisation value is lost if they know the answer in advance. _source: ch12 §The Payoff Profiles Matrix in practice, Tip 2_

**Rule 3: Multi-colour Post-its.** Use different Post-it colours to distinguish starting initiatives from improved versions. This makes each initiative's glide path visually trackable on the wall. _source: ch12 §The Payoff Profiles Matrix in practice, Tip 3_

**Rule 4: Digital app threshold.** Use physical Post-its for sessions with ≤15 initiatives. Use the digital app at www.strategic.how/payoff when there are more than 15 initiatives, when you need to easily capture evolving versions, or when displaying a full glide path. _source: ch12 §The Payoff Profiles Matrix in practice, Tip 4_

**Rule 5: Words over numbers.** The matrix relies on words, not financial models or quantitative scoring. This enables 10–20 people across functions to participate, bringing in the full breadth of project knowledge, flair, and organisational expertise. _source: ch12 §Payoff Profiles example_

## Resilience and Revisiting

Initiatives that were once world-shaping erode over time into Adapt or even Reserve positions as competitors catch up. **Every few years — or months in fast-moving industries — the position of initiatives will have shifted and should be revisited** to drag them leftward again. _source: ch12 §Payoff Profiles example_

Schedule a matrix review as part of your annual strategy cycle. Use the Russian Dolls Quality to cascade the review across levels.

## Anti-Patterns

### Reserve Big Bet (the named danger zone)
Committing to a high-risk, high-cost initiative while the strategic posture is merely to reserve the right to play — yielding maximum risk for minimal differentiated reward.

**Signals:**
- The initiative is expensive AND has a high probability of failure
- Even in the best-case scenario, the outcome is no different from what competitors already offer
- The initiative is plotted in the top-right corner

**Fix:** Transform it using the Destroy decision and a glide path — move it toward Options, toward Adapt, or both simultaneously. _source: ch12 §Destroy_

### Jumping Ahead with the First Version of an Idea
Executing the initial conception of a strategic initiative without first engineering its payoff profile.

**Signals:**
- No structured risk–reward assessment before committing resources
- The team is "moving fast and breaking things" without strategic validation

**Fix:** Apply the Payoff Profiles Matrix before committing. Use a glide path to iterate from the starting position to a more favourable risk–reward combination. _source: ch12 §Payoff Profiles exercise_

## When to Use / When NOT to Use

**Use the Payoff Profiles Matrix when:**
- You have 5–30 strategic initiatives to classify and prioritise
- You want to run a participatory portfolio session with 10–20 people across functions
- The team is debating resource allocation without a shared framework
- You are entering the Down phase of the Rollercoaster and need to assess which bets to place

**Do NOT use when:**
- You have fewer than 3–4 initiatives (the matrix adds overhead with no comparative value)
- You need precise financial modelling — this is a words-based, directional tool, not a capital allocation spreadsheet
- The team has not yet agreed on the initiative list (run a Pyramid Principle or Happy Line session first to surface and structure ideas)
- You are in the Push phase — by then, portfolio decisions should already be made

## Code Examples

The Payoff Profiles Matrix is a facilitation tool, not a software pattern. The canonical representation is the 3×3 grid itself, labelled exactly as the book specifies.

**The 3×3 Grid (text representation):**

```
                  POSTURES (Reward)
                  Shape   Adapt   Reserve
               ┌───────┬───────┬──────────┐
  Big Bets     │ DISCUSS│       │ DESTROY  │
               │       │       │(empty!)  │
BETS  ├───────┼───────┼──────────┤
(Risk) Options │ DRAG → │ DRAG →│ DRAG →  │
               │ target │       │          │
               ├───────┼───────┼──────────┤
  No Regrets   │DELEGATE│DELEGATE│DELEGATE │
               └───────┴───────┴──────────┘
                          ↑ Target: Shape Options
```

**Session workflow (step-by-step):**

```
Step 1  Label the Initiatives (5 min)
        → Compile all current initiatives
        → Give each a catchy name of 1–4 words

Step 2  Plot the Initiatives (10 min)
        → Draw the 3×3 grid on a wall or whiteboard
        → Simultaneous vote (rock-paper-scissors style)
          on BOTH axes for each initiative, ≤30 sec each
        → Plot consensus items first; debate contested
          placements in a second round

Step 3  Take Stock and Adjust (5 min)
        → Review for misplacements relative to neighbours
        → Adjust to match group consensus

Step 4  Delegate
        → Remove all No Regret initiatives from the grid
        → Assign to junior team members

Step 5  Destroy
        → For each Reserve Big Bet:
            Option A: move DOWN  → reduce to staged investment
            Option B: move LEFT  → redesign for higher reward
            Option C: move BOTH  → glide path to Shape Options
        → Use a new Post-it colour for each improved version

Step 6  Discuss
        → Debate each Shape Big Bet at the senior level
        → Can it be restructured as an Option?

Step 7  Drag
        → Engineer improved versions of all remaining
          initiatives, moving each toward Shape Options
        → Plot each improved version with a new Post-it colour

Step 8  (Optional) Use www.strategic.how/payoff
        → Required if >15 initiatives or displaying glide paths
```

**Transforming a Reserve Big Bet (glide path):**

```
Starting position:  Reserve Big Bet  (top-right)
                         │
      ┌──────────────────┼──────────────────┐
      ▼                  ▼                  ▼
  Move DOWN          Move LEFT          Move BOTH
  Reserve Option  →  Adapt Big Bet  →  Adapt Option
  (stage-gate        (higher reward     (best of
   investment)        redesign)          both)
                         │
                         ▼
              Target: Shape Options
              (or as close as achievable)
```

_source: ch12 §Destroy, §The Payoff Profiles Matrix in practice_
