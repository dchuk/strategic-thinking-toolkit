# Happy Line Framework

## Problem Framing

You're entering a planning session and everyone around the table has opinions about what customers want. Some teammates push for better product quality; others argue for lower prices; the marketing team wants to invest in brand. Nobody agrees on what to prioritise, and no one has data good enough to settle the debate.

The underlying problem is not a lack of data — it's a lack of a shared, visual way to map what customers value most against what your organisation actually delivers. The result is misallocated investment: over-delivering on criteria customers barely care about while under-delivering on the ones that drive purchase decisions.

The Happy Line framework (an Up tool, used in the **Up** phase of the Rollercoaster to generate strategic ideas) solves this by giving a team a single flipchart and 60 minutes to produce a collectively owned diagnostic and a first wave of actionable ideas — without needing validated market research first.

---

## What the Happy Line Is

The Happy Line is a "lazy L" (hockey-stick-shaped) curve plotted on a two-dimensional graph. The horizontal axis lists **Key Purchasing Criteria (KPCs)** ranked in decreasing order of importance, most important on the left. The vertical axis shows customer-perceived supplier performance on each criterion.

The curve represents the minimum level of satisfaction a supplier must reach on each KPC to succeed in the market. The prescription: be great on the most important criteria, merely good on the less important ones — not uniformly excellent across everything. _source: ch09 §The Happy Line of customer satisfaction_

> "Satisfaction is maximised by being great on the most important criteria and merely good on the less important ones — not by scoring high on everything."

The Happy Line originated in Professor Chan Kim's **Blue Ocean Strategy** work and was adapted into this session-based facilitation format.

---

## The Three Zones

Once the Lazy-L is drawn, every KPC Post-it falls into one of three zones:

| Zone | Location on Graph | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Front Zone** | Bottom-left | High-importance KPCs, underperforming | Priority improvement area |
| **Top Zone** | Top-right | Low-importance KPCs, over-delivering | Resource can be traded off |
| **Back Zone** | Bottom-right | Low-importance KPCs, underperforming | Improve, but don't sacrifice higher-priority KPCs |

> "The marketing zone is usually the top right of the graph; the strategy zone is the bottom left. The Happy Line takes from the marketing zone and recycles resources towards the strategy zone." _source: ch09 §Happy Line example (trade-offs step)_

---

## N-Post-it Ideas: The Core Ideation Unit

Ideas generated from a Happy Line are categorised by the number of KPC Post-its they simultaneously move closer to the Happy Line.

- **1-Post-it idea** — moves a single KPC toward the line (usually a Front-zone criterion upward)
- **2-Post-its idea** — moves two KPCs: typically improves a Front-zone criterion while slightly reducing a Top-zone criterion (a trade-off)
- **3-Post-its, 4-Post-its** — more complex ideas touching multiple KPCs simultaneously

> "Ideas with more Post-its are subtler, less obvious, and more likely to succeed." _source: ch09 §Happy Line example (idea generation)_

**Rule 5 (clockwise movement):** A legitimate improvement idea must make Post-its move clockwise — left-side Post-its go up (Front zone improves), right-side Post-its go slightly down (Top zone is deliberately reduced). An idea that moves Post-its anti-clockwise is not a valid strategic trade-off. _source: ch09 §Happy Line in practice – Tip 2_

## The 60-Minute Happy Line Session

### Setup (1 min)

Take a flipchart page (portrait) and split it in two horizontally.
- **Top half:** Draw axes — horizontal = KPCs (arrow pointing left, most important first); vertical = customer rating. Do NOT draw the Lazy-L yet.
- **Bottom half:** Draw four quadrants labelled **1, 2, 3, 4+** for idea capture by N-Post-it count.
- Choose Stakeholder X (customers, employees, boss, regulators, etc.).

### Assess Stakeholder X (10 min)

Distribute ownership across six roles to produce a collectively owned diagnostic:

1. **Person 1** writes 10–12 KPC Post-its (team suggests freely)
2. **Person 2** reviews and may change up to two Post-its
3. **Person 3** ranks KPCs in decreasing importance and eliminates the least important, keeping **six to eight**
4. **Person 4** may swap the ranking of up to two remaining Post-its
5. **Person 5** rates the entity's performance on each KPC as Stakeholder X would perceive it
6. **Person 6** may shift up to two Post-its vertically

Label each Post-it with a letter (a, b, c, …) in the bottom-right corner.

> "The Happy Line diagnostic should be a collective achievement so that nobody is particularly attached to it and nobody is too defensive about it." _source: ch09 §Happy Line example (six-person process)_

### Draw the Happy Line

Place the Lazy-L inflection at the position (roughly versions 3 or 4 out of 5 possible positions) that broadly splits the Post-its into two batches of roughly equal importance, maximising available trade-offs. _source: ch09 §Happy Line example (where to draw the line)_

- Do NOT draw a completely horizontal line at 10/10 (version 1) — implies failure everywhere, no useful output.
- Do NOT draw a line that drops too steeply (version 5) — implies excellence everywhere, no useful trade-offs.

### Generate Ideas (30 min)

- **Box 1:** Three to four 1-Post-it ideas that move the most critical KPC up toward the line.
- **Box 2:** Three to four 2-Post-its ideas trading off a Top-zone KPC to improve a Front-zone KPC.
- **Boxes 3 & 4+:** Continue with 3-Post-its and 4-Post-its ideas.

Write each idea on a different-colour Post-it. Include in brackets the letters of the KPCs the idea alters (e.g., `One less train daily (a, e)`).

> "Begin idea generation with a comprehensive list of '1-Post-it' and '2-Post-its' ideas before progressing to three, four, or more." _source: ch09 §Happy Line in practice – Tip 4_

### Package Your Best Ideas (15 min)

Summarise the best ideas in two to four words each. Include the component KPC letters for clarity.

### Optional: Individual Diagnostic Rounds

After the group session, have each team member independently create their own version of the diagnostic on a separate flipchart and extract ideas from each version.

> "Ideas common to all versions are especially robust because good ideas address many problems at once." _source: ch09 §Happy Line example (individual versions)_

## Key Rules

**Rule 1:** Plot KPCs on the horizontal axis in decreasing order of importance, most important on the left. _source: ch09 §The Happy Line of customer satisfaction_

**Rule 2:** After identifying 10–12 initial KPCs, eliminate the least important ones to retain only the top **six to eight**. _source: ch09 §Happy Line in practice – Assess Stakeholder X step_

**Rule 3:** Do not merge two high-importance KPCs into one. If any top-four or top-five KPC contains a sub-criterion independently important enough to make the top seven, spin it off as a separate Post-it. _source: ch09 §Happy Line in practice – Tip 1_

**Rule 4:** Draw the Happy Line so that it broadly splits Post-its into two batches of equal importance, maximising available trade-offs. _source: ch09 §Happy Line example (where to draw the line)_

**Rule 5:** A legitimate improvement idea must make Post-its move **clockwise** (left side up, right side slightly down). _source: ch09 §Happy Line in practice – Tip 2_

**Rule 6:** Label each KPC Post-it with a letter (a, b, c, …) and reference those letters on idea Post-its. _source: ch09 §Happy Line in practice – Tip 4_

**Rule 7:** Spend **five to ten minutes** plotting the Post-its, then spend **fifty to fifty-five minutes** extracting and packaging ideas. _source: ch09 §Happy Line example (time allocation advice)_

**Rule 8:** For market entry diagnostics — first perform the Happy Line at market level; if the market leader is close to the line, split into three or four segments and perform a Happy Line for each to find the segment where the incumbent is furthest from the line. _source: ch09 §Happy Line example (diagnostic step)_

## Beyond Corporate Strategy

The Happy Line applies to any stakeholder relationship. The same flipchart format works for:

- **Employees** (what do my team members need from me as a manager?)
- **Boss** (what matters to my manager in a direct report?)
- **Suppliers / regulators / shareholders**
- **Personal relationships** (family members, partners)

> "Don't treat people as you would like to be treated; treat them as they would like to be treated." _source: ch09 §Away from corporate strategy paragraph_

**Boss Happy Line — quick workflow:**
1. Ask: "What's important to my boss in a direct report?" List as KPCs.
2. Rank in decreasing order of importance on the horizontal axis.
3. For each KPC, ask: "How well would my boss say I'm doing on this?" Plot performance.
4. Draw the Lazy-L, splitting Post-its evenly.
5. Generate 1-Post-it, 2-Post-its ideas for specific actions to optimise the relationship.
6. Implement. If the boss responds positively, the idea was good. If not, try alternatives or redo the diagnostic.

## Code Examples

### Annotated Happy Line Flipchart Layout

```
HAPPY LINE SESSION — FLIPCHART LAYOUT (portrait)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

TOP HALF — Diagnostic
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
10 |         ·
   |      ·     ·                            ← Top Zone
   |           ___________
   |          /            (inflection point ~KPC 3–4)
   |_________/
   |  ·   ·                                  ← Front Zone
 0 +──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   (a)   (b)   (c)   (d)   (e)   (f)   (g)   (h)
 most important KPC ──────────────────► least important KPC

KPC Post-its labelled a–h in bottom-right corner.
Dots = supplier performance on each KPC.
Lazy-L drawn so roughly equal Post-its fall above and below.

BOTTOM HALF — Ideas by N-Post-it Count
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
┌──────────────────┬──────────────────┐
│     Box 1        │     Box 2        │
│  1-Post-it ideas │ 2-Post-its ideas │
│ (improve one KPC)│ (trade-off pair) │
├──────────────────┼──────────────────┤
│     Box 3        │     Box 4+       │
│ 3-Post-its ideas │ 4+ Post-its ideas│
│                  │ (complex/subtle) │
└──────────────────┴──────────────────┘

Example idea Post-its:
  Box 1: "Add live tracking  (a)"
  Box 2: "One less train daily  (a↑, e↓)"
  Box 4: "Express + app bundle  (a↑, b↑, e↓, f↓)"
```

### Market Entry Diagnostic — Step Sequence

```
MARKET ENTRY HAPPY LINE — STEP SEQUENCE
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Step 1: Market-level Happy Line
  → Plot market leader's performance on all KPCs
  → Draw Lazy-L

Step 2: Gap check
  Is market leader close to the Happy Line overall?
  ├── NO  → Large immediate opportunity identified; go to Step 4
  └── YES → Proceed to Step 3 (segment analysis)

Step 3: Segment split
  → Split market into 3–4 segments
  → Draw a separate Happy Line for each segment
  → Identify the segment where the incumbent is FURTHEST from line
  → That segment = entry point

Step 4: Zone-based idea generation
  Front Zone (bottom-left):
    → Generate ideas to dramatically outperform incumbent
      on the most important KPCs in this segment

  Top Zone (top-right):
    → Identify KPCs where incumbent over-invests
    → Plan to redirect those resources to Front Zone

Step 5: Cost-structure check
  → Shifting from fixed-cost KPCs (R&D, production)
    to variable-cost KPCs (service) reduces upfront risk
```

## When to Use / When NOT to Use

### Use the Happy Line when:

- You need to identify strategic trade-offs between what customers value most and current supplier performance
- You are entering a new market and want to find underserved or over-served KPCs
- You are the incumbent and want to identify your Achilles heel segment
- You want to improve a relationship with any stakeholder — customers, employees, suppliers, regulators, shareholders, boss, or family
- You have a cross-functional team and need a fast, collectively owned diagnostic
- You want to generate customer-centric improvement ideas quickly (under 60 minutes)

_source: ch09 §Away from corporate strategy paragraph and §Happy Line example_

### Do NOT use the Happy Line when:

- You require a precise, data-validated market research output before taking any action. The Happy Line is designed to generate ideas without requiring real data; it is not a substitute for rigorous quantitative research.

> "A great solution trumps an accurate diagnostic all the time." _source: ch09 §Happy Line example (data not required)_
> "Always spend more money testing solutions than on validating the original diagnostic." _source: ch09 §Happy Line example (research vs. testing)_

---

## Anti-Patterns to Watch For

**Over-investing in low-importance KPCs** — Spending resources on Top-zone criteria while Front-zone criteria go unaddressed. Fix: identify Top-zone Post-its, accept reduced performance there, redirect resources to the Front zone. _source: ch09 §The Happy Line of customer satisfaction_

**Spending too long on the diagnostic, too little on ideas** — Teams that debate KPC ordering for 20+ minutes routinely run out of time before generating a single idea. Fix: timebox Post-it plotting to five to ten minutes; protect 50+ minutes for idea generation. _source: ch09 §Happy Line example (time allocation advice)_

**Clustering high-importance KPCs together** (Merging Top KPCs) — Combining two distinct high-importance criteria into one Post-it reduces trade-off granularity. Fix: resist clustering in the top four or five; spin off sub-criteria as independent Post-its if they are independently important. _source: ch09 §Happy Line in practice – Tip 1_

**Stopping at 'improve A' as an idea** — Vague improvement statements ("improve punctuality") are not actionable ideas. Fix: treat any "improve X" statement as a starting point only, then push immediately for at least five or six specific mechanisms to achieve it. _source: ch09 §Happy Line in practice – Tip 3_

For the full catalog of named anti-patterns across all phases, see [Anti-Patterns Catalog](../anti-patterns/anti-patterns-catalog.md).
