# Impactful Words

## Problem Framing

You have done the thinking. You have the data, the strategy, and the plan. And yet the room stays cold, the executive is unconvinced, and the initiative stalls.

The most common culprit is not the quality of the argument — it is the *format and nature* of the words used to deliver it. A strategist who always leads with spreadsheets will lose the kinaesthetic executive who needs to feel the idea. One who relies exclusively on logical Reason will never move the colleague who responds to Inspiration. And the executive summary that runs to three pages gets folded in half and forgotten.

Impactful Words is a **Push-phase tool** — used after the Up and Down phases have produced a recommendation. Its job is to ensure the recommendation actually lands.

Two complementary frameworks do this work: **NLP Representation Systems** (the *format* of persuasive words) and **10 Ways to Convince** (the *nature* of those words). _source: ch16 §10 Ways to Convince (opening paragraph)_

## NLP Representation Systems

People have a strong preference for two of four channels through which they receive and process information. Presenting information in someone's preferred channel makes it hit harder and land faster; presenting it in their least-favourite channel makes it feel less convincing and takes longer to absorb. _source: ch16 §NLP Language_

### The Four Channels

| Channel | Preference | Characteristic vocabulary |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual** | Seeing, watching | see, focus, clear, bright, picture, hazy |
| **Auditory** | Hearing, speaking | sound, hear, tell, say, click, bang |
| **Kinaesthetic** | Touching, feeling (also smell/taste) | impact, feel, grasp, tense, rough, relax |
| **Digital** | Reading, counting facts and data | accommodate, capability, comprehend, structure |

_source: ch16 §VISUAL SYSTEM, §AUDITORY SYSTEM, §KINAESTHETIC SYSTEM, §DIGITAL SYSTEM_

### How to Identify a Person's Channel

**Rule 1: Observe before approaching.** Before initiating a high-stakes persuasion conversation, observe the person from afar — how they walk, dress, and interact — for early channel clues. _source: ch16 §NLP Language example (Audi showroom)_

**Rule 2: Listen to their vocabulary.** In early conversation, categorise the words they use. Frequency of channel-specific vocabulary reveals their top one or two preferred channels.

**Rule 3: Adapt your medium, not just your words.** Visual → slides and pictures. Auditory → spoken dialogue and voice-led conversation. Kinaesthetic → hands-on experiences and emotion. Digital → structured written data and brochures.

**Rule 4: Blend all four channels for mixed audiences.** The best way to convince a varied audience is to blend all four representation systems in your presentation, especially in the executive summary. _source: ch16 §NLP Language example (showroom section end)_

### Anti-Pattern: Own-Channel Bias

Presenting information only through your own preferred channel regardless of the audience. Old-fashioned salespeople, for example, deliver long verbal explanations (auditory) to every customer — losing the visual buyer who wants to see it and the digital buyer who wants to read the spec sheet.

**Fix:** Observe vocabulary and behaviour to identify the audience's preferred channel(s), then adapt language, media, and interaction style to match. _source: ch16 §NLP Language example (Audi showroom)_

## 10 Ways to Convince

The **10 Ways to Convince** model names ten distinct methods of persuasion. Most people habitually use only three or four, leaving entire categories of people unpersuaded because those people respond to different methods. _source: ch16 §10 Ways to Convince_

### The Ten Methods

**Head-focused (rational/structural):**

1. **Reason** — logical argument supported by evidence
2. **Ask** — questions that lead recipients to convince themselves; builds ownership
3. **Authority** — time-pressured directive from a position of power
4. **Force** — compliance demanded when all else has failed (use sparingly)
5. **Expertise** — track record and prior experience with similar problems

**Heart-focused (emotional/relational):**

6. **Inspire** — emotional energy and vision, especially for future-oriented initiatives
7. **Silent Allies** — benchmarking and peer comparison to reassure a cautious audience
8. **Feel Good** — goodwill-building; softens resistance in the relationship
9. **Deal** — mutual exchange in a political or reciprocal environment
10. **Favour** — appealing to kindness to remove early heat from a conversation

_source: ch16 §10 Ways to Convince_

### Broadening Your Range

**Principle:** Strategists should broaden their active range from the typical three or four preferred ways toward all ten. *"Would you rather drive a four-cylinder car or an eight-cylinder car? Most people work on four cylinders. If you can broaden that to eight or 10, that would be extraordinary."* _source: ch16 §10 Ways to Convince_

**Workflow — Broaden Your Convincing Repertoire:**

1. List all ten methods.
2. Identify your three to four most frequently used methods (your default cylinders).
3. Identify the methods you rarely or never use.
4. For each underused method, find a friend, colleague, or relative who naturally uses it effectively.
5. Observe and practise mimicking their approach in low-stakes interactions.
6. Deliberately deploy one unfamiliar method in your next persuasion situation and observe whether it works.
7. Gradually expand your active repertoire toward all ten methods.

_source: ch16 §10 Ways to Convince (final paragraphs)_

### Anti-Pattern: Narrow Convincing Repertoire

Relying habitually on only three or four methods. Signals: always defaulting to Reason; never using Inspire, Feel Good, or Favour in professional settings; finding certain colleagues impossible to convince.

**Fix:** Identify least-used methods, practise them by mimicking colleagues who use them naturally, and consciously deploy a wider range. _source: ch16 §10 Ways to Convince_

## One-Page Executive Summary (Flyer Format)

The Push phase often culminates in a written summary that must grab a busy decision-maker in under one second. The Flyer Format structures this summary like a great flyer — not like a consultant report.

### The Four Rules

**Rule 1: One page only.** Not two, not three. _source: ch16 §Impactful Words exercise: How to pitch a new venture?_

**Rule 2: 200–300 words maximum.** Body text must stay within this band — enough to inform, not enough to overwhelm. _source: ch16 §Impactful Words exercise: How to pitch a new venture?_

**Rule 3: Highlight key text.** Readers read diagonally and need visual cues to guide them. Highlight key phrases and data points throughout. _source: ch16 §Impactful Words exercise: How to pitch a new venture?_

**Rule 4: Exactly three key metrics.** Include upfront investment required, a key operational success metric, and a financial return metric — no more, no fewer. _source: ch16 §Impactful Words exercise: How to pitch a new venture?_

### Writing the Flyer — Step by Step

1. Constrain yourself to exactly one page.
2. Place a compelling slogan in large, bold text near the top (e.g. *'Meds On Tap'* for an online pharmacy).
3. Include one stunning, memorable photo that visually anchors the concept.
4. Add super-short bios of the two to four key people who will execute the plan.
5. Include exactly three key metrics: investment required, key operational metric, projected financial return.
6. Write body text in 200–300 words.
7. Highlight key phrases and data points to guide diagonal readers.
8. Review: a reader should grasp the core message in under one second of scanning.

_source: ch16 §Impactful Words exercise: How to pitch a new venture?_

### Anti-Pattern: Multi-Page Executive Summary

Writing a two-or-three-page summary forces recipients to flip pages and loses their attention before the key message lands. Signals: executive summary exceeds one page; no visual hierarchy or highlights; more than 300 words of body text.

**Fix:** Condense to the Flyer Format above. _source: ch16 §Impactful Words exercise: How to pitch a new venture?_

## When to Use / When NOT to Use

**Use Impactful Words when:**
- You are entering the Push phase and need to turn a completed analysis into a persuasive recommendation.
- You are preparing for a high-stakes one-on-one conversation and know (or can infer) the other person's preferred representation channel.
- You are addressing a mixed audience and need to maximise the chance of convincing everyone present.
- You are writing an executive summary or new-venture pitch for a busy decision-maker.
- You have noticed that certain colleagues or stakeholders remain consistently unconvinced by your arguments — a signal your convincing repertoire is too narrow.

**Do NOT use (or adapt) when:**
- The audience has explicitly requested a detailed multi-page report — the one-page rule bends to explicit stakeholder requirements.
- You are still in the Up or Down phase; invest that time in building the right strategy first. Impactful Words polishes and delivers, it does not substitute for Clarity or Certainty.
- You already know the audience's channel preferences deeply (long-term relationship) — the observe-first workflow can be abbreviated, but the adaptation principle still applies.
- Do not use Authority with senior board executives or audiences who will not accept it. _source: ch16 §10 Ways to Convince_

## Code Examples

The following tables illustrate the NLP channel vocabulary and 10 Ways to Convince model as applied in practice.

### Matching Channel to Audience

```
Situation: Pitching a new logistics platform to a mixed leadership team.

Visual executive (uses "picture", "clear", "I see"):
  → Open with a one-slide visual map of the current vs. future state.
  → Use: "Let me show you what this looks like in practice."

Auditory executive (uses "sounds right", "tell me more", "I hear you"):
  → Walk them verbally through the narrative before showing any slide.
  → Use: "Let me talk you through the story from the customer's point of view."

Kinaesthetic executive (uses "feels right", "I can grasp that", "rough idea"):
  → Bring a physical prototype or role-play the customer experience.
  → Use: "I'd like you to feel what the driver experiences at the handover point."

Digital executive (uses "capability", "comprehend", "specifically"):
  → Lead with a structured written brief and supporting data appendix.
  → Use: "Here is the structured breakdown of the four capability requirements."

For the whole room: open with the visual map, walk through the narrative,
invite them to role-play one scenario, and close with the three-metric summary.
```

```
  [ ] Feel Good   [ ] Deal        [ ] Favour

  These are your missing cylinders.

Step 3 – For each missing method, name one colleague who uses it naturally:
  Inspire   → _______________
  Feel Good → _______________
  Favour    → _______________
  (etc.)

Step 4 – Commit to deploying one unfamiliar method in your next meeting.
  Target method: _______________
  Target audience signal that it might work: _______________
```
